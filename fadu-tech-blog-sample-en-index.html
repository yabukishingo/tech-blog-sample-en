Processing Thousands of Orders Per Second

Because Fint operates as a discretionary investment service, every account requires distinct orders with different tickers and quantities. If 100,000 accounts trade 10 stocks each in a single day, the system must process at least 1 million orders—each with varying methods and conditions.

Executing trades involves far more complexity than standard limit or after-hours orders. Limit orders branch into conditional, best, and top-priority limit orders, while market orders span regular, pre-market, and after-hours sessions.

Beyond order types, the engine must navigate strict execution options for every individual account:

  • FAS (Fill and Store): Fills the immediately available quantity and keeps the remainder open on the order book until market close.
  • IOC (Immediate or Cancel): Executes whatever can be filled immediately and cancels the rest.
  • FOK (Fill or Kill): Executes the entire order immediately or cancels it completely.

Relying solely on market orders guarantees immediate execution, but buying at unfavorable prices erodes client returns over time. Conversely, waiting passively for limit orders risks missing target allocations altogether. PREFACE evaluates every possible scenario and account status in real time, leveraging parallel processing to maintain peak throughput.

Scaling up with more human operators to mimic parallel processing would drive up labor costs—and ultimately client fees—while increasing the risk of human error. Managing millions of accounts simultaneously with real-time filtering is virtually impossible without an automated engine like PREFACE.

Testing: The Cornerstone of Order Stability

Every order on Fint passes through partner brokerage firms. To execute ISAAC’s target portfolio, PREFACE must align with exchange trading rules and communicate with brokerages via API calls tailored to each firm's specific order protocol.

The real challenge arises when either Fint or a partner brokerage updates its system. If a protocol change isn't reflected accurately, it can trigger critical failures—such as rejected orders or incorrect prices and quantities. This leaves clients with off-strategy portfolios, requiring time-consuming rebalancing that directly hurts returns.

The only way to prevent this is rigorous testing. Fint built a dedicated testing sandbox within PREFACE that mirrors our live production environment. This allows us to repeatedly validate each brokerage’s protocol and collect scenario-based order data to prepare for edge cases before they happen.

Real-Time Order Monitoring System

On days of extreme market volatility, orders may not fill as intended. PREFACE’s real-time monitoring system continuously tracks order statuses across brokerages and exchanges. If an order remains unfilled, the system cancels it, reassesses market conditions, and places a more advantageous re-order.

Furthermore, if an unexpected account or brokerage issue occurs—such as a dormant account or a security lock triggered by unauthorized access—PREFACE immediately halts the trading strategy for that account. An alert is sent to administrators in real time so they can resolve the issue and safely resume execution. Ultimately, this monitoring system ensures every trade strictly adheres to ISAAC’s strategy, protecting the client’s portfolio experience.

Automated Post-Trade Reconciliation

Once the market closes, PREFACE initiates automated reconciliation with partner brokerages—much like balancing the books at the end of a business day. The system cross-checks every single executed trade across all accounts to catch any potential discrepancies on the brokerage or exchange side.

If a mismatch is detected, PREFACE pinpoints the root cause, determines the recovery path, and requests verification from the brokerage if needed. Achieving 100% data synchronization through daily reconciliation is critical to preventing disruptions when the market opens the next morning.
